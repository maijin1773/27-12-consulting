## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment (Assessment A):** The copy and hero photography are genuinely specific — marketplace/fashion vocabulary (SKU, ABC-анализ, юнит-экономика, P&L, процент выкупа) and a stark editorial hero photo couldn't drop into a law firm or SaaS site unchanged. But the *structural* system (navy/cream alternation, gold accent, pill buttons, hairline cards) is a well-built, generic "boutique premium services" shell that could dress a styling agency or wedding planner with a copy-and-photo swap. The system calls itself "The Trend Auditor" with a ledger/audit metaphor, but nothing on the page actually *looks* like a ledger, an SKU table, or a price/margin chart — the specificity lives in words and one photo, not in composition.

## Overall Impression

The bones are unusually disciplined for a template-era landing page — a documented design system, a correctly-implemented "inverted card" rule, thorough `prefers-reduced-motion` coverage, and copy that actually speaks the audience's language. But the system's own two strictest rules — one accent color, one serif — are both violated at scale, and a sixth undocumented typeface (Unbounded) shows up in the first section after the hero. That's very likely the concrete, nameable reason the site "doesn't feel modern": not a missing trend, but a self-declared restraint that isn't actually being kept anywhere except the hero.

## What's Working

1. **Hero split panel** (`index.html:61-80`) — full-bleed black-and-white photography against oversized Playfair Display with one italic gold word is genuinely on-brand and editorial. The one place the "Trend Auditor" identity is fully realized.
2. **The Inverted Card Rule is real, not just documented** — format tiles, service cards, project columns, stage cards, and the steps list all correctly invert against their section's background, consistently, everywhere.
3. **`prefers-reduced-motion` is honored comprehensively** — marquee, curtain-reveal, fold-heading, letter-reveal, tilt-cards, and the team photo fan-out all have guarded fallbacks. More thorough than most professional builds.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] An undocumented 6th typeface breaks the type system right after the hero.**
`css/style.css:920` — `.format-label, .format-tile h3, .format-price, .format-link { font-family: 'Unbounded', sans-serif; }`, loaded at `index.html:10`. DESIGN.md documents exactly five fonts and calls the character "editorial feature, audit report underneath." Unbounded is a heavy geometric grotesque associated with crypto/SaaS branding — in "Три формата работы," the very first section after the hero, it reads like a different site got pasted in.
- **Fix:** swap to Oswald (labels) / Golos Text (titles) per the documented hierarchy.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable typeset`

**[P0] The One Accent Rule is violated at scale, everywhere.**
Gold (`--gold`) colors `.format-tile h3` (×3), `.service-price` (×6), `.team-card h3` (×3), `.stage-number` (×3), `.step-num` (×4), plus the logo numeral. DESIGN.md says gold appears "at most once or twice per screen." In practice it's the default color for every number and every card title — a generic highlight utility, not a rare accent. Restraint in accent color is one of the strongest signals of expensive design; this site has none left to spend.
- **Fix:** recolor service-price, team-card names, and format-tile titles to the standard on-card text color; keep gold on the hero word, the logo, and at most one numeral-type per section.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable colorize`

**[P1] The team pin-track forces ~1500px of extra mobile scroll with no payoff.**
`css/style.css:716` — `.team-pin-track { height: calc(100vh + 1500px); }` is not reduced at the 900px/600px breakpoints, even though `js/script.js:107-111` already defines separate `PILE_MOBILE` values (acknowledging mobile needs different treatment, but only for the pose, not the track length). On a ~700px phone this is roughly 3 screens of scrolling that reveal no new content — just 3 photos slowly un-piling.
- **Fix:** shorten the track height under the same breakpoint that already redefines the pile values, or drop the scroll-pin behavior below ~900px for a static grid.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

**[P1] Team photography is three unrelated photo shoots standing in as "the team."**
`team-nadezhda.jpg` (moody warm-gray wall, candid), `team-ksenia.jpg` (bright white studio, seated), `team-maria.jpg` (lifestyle home interior, plants, window light) — three different backdrops, color temperatures, and framing logics. With zero case studies or testimonials on the site by product design, team photography is the primary human trust signal a first-time skeptical visitor scans — right now it reads as three individually-submitted photos, not one coordinated bureau.
- **Fix:** at minimum, uniformly grade and crop (same backdrop tone, same aspect logic) so the trio reads as one team.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

**[P2] Six-plus simultaneous offer cards contradict the section's own promise.**
`index.html:167-235` — "Начни с одного шага" (start with **one** step) is immediately followed by 6 fully-detailed cards (zone/title/price/description/format/CTA) plus a 7th promo block with its own 4-item list — 6-7 parallel decision points against the ≤4 working-memory guideline.
- **Fix:** group into the 3 zones already established by the format tiles (Ассортимент / Кабинет / Контент), or collapse price/format behind light disclosure.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

**[P3] Dead CSS and an ambiguous variable name invite future accent-rule violations.**
`.format-price` is fully styled (`css/style.css:918, 929-930, 956-958`) but no `.format-tile` in the current HTML renders a price element — leftover from a changed content model. Separately, `--accent: #f2f2e8` (`css/style.css:6`) is bone-cream, not gold, despite `--gold` being the actual documented accent — an ambiguous name in exactly the kind of place that produced the P0 above.
- **Fix:** remove the dead `.format-price` rule; rename `--accent` to something unambiguous (e.g. `--on-navy`).
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

**[P3] Em-dash saturation flagged by the detector (advisory).**
14 em-dashes across body copy, file-wide (no specific line localized by the regex-mode detector) — flagged as a density pattern associated with AI-generated cadence. Advisory only; verify against actual sentences before rewriting anything, since the underlying copy is stated as real, human-authored business content.
- **Fix:** spot-check body copy for em-dash density; replace some with commas/periods where it reads more natural.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

Worth stress-testing specifically for text selection, in-page search (Ctrl+F), and copy-paste behavior on price and service-name text — exactly the strings a prospect might want to copy and forward to a colleague.

**Casey (distracted mobile):** Covered above under P1 — the mobile team section costs ~3 screens of scroll for 3 photos settling into place, with no shortened path for a distracted, one-handed visitor.

## Minor Observations

- Buttons (`.btn`, `.btn-primary`, `.btn-ghost`, `.btn-on-navy`) have no custom `:focus-visible` style — falls back to the browser default outline, which will visually clash with the cream/navy palette even though it stays functional.
- The hero logo "handwriting" SVG animation (`css/style.css:503-629`) loops infinitely directly over the hero photo — a persistent motion element competing with the photography that DESIGN.md itself says should be "the loudest thing on the page."
- No skip-to-content link before the sticky header + marquee ticker, for keyboard users.
- `.service-price` gold-on-navy contrast checks out at roughly 5.3:1 (passes AA) by hand calculation — flagged as a non-issue, but worth confirming since the deterministic scan couldn't verify computed contrast in degraded mode.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the system calls itself "The Trend Auditor" with a ledger/audit metaphor, why does no element on the page actually look like a table, an SKU list, or a price/margin chart — the literal tools this bureau sells?
2. Gold is documented as "at most once or twice per screen" but currently colors every price, every stage number, every step number, and every team member's name — at what point does an accent color that's everywhere stop being an accent at all?
3. With zero testimonials by product design, is the current build asking its least-consistent asset — three unrelated team photo shoots — to carry the trust-building work that social proof would normally do?
